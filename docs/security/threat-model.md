# Threat model: voice → Gemini → database

Status: voice, the parser, the Gemini fallback (`/api/parse`) and the AI coach (`/api/coach`, with Groq as a second provider) are built, with fake-provider tests (`api/_lib/parse.test.ts`, `coach.test.ts`, `coachScope.test.ts`, `fallbackProvider.test.ts`; `/api/parse` was checked once against the live API and `npm run check:coach` passed for both providers). Cloud speech-to-text (`/api/transcribe`, Groq, [ADR-019](../architecture/decisions/019-cloud-speech-to-text.md)) is built and tested with fakes (`api/_lib/transcribe.test.ts`, `transcribeProvider.test.ts`); it was checked on an iPhone 15 in Safari and Chrome against the deployed site. All of T1 to T20 apply (T7 and T16 are controls in process and key handling rather than tested code).

Scope: the data path from the microphone to Postgres, plus auth and dependencies. Drafted with AI assistance and reviewed by the developer; update as the system is built.

## Assets

User workout data (their own, low sensitivity but personal), Supabase session tokens, the Gemini API key, the free-tier quotas, and the integrity of stored numbers.

## Trust boundaries

1. **Microphone → recogniser.** Chrome's Web Speech API sends audio to Google's servers for recognition; Safari uses Apple's. Audio leaves the device. We cannot control this; we document it. Where the browser's recogniser ends with no words (iPhone), or the device is in cloud mode, the app records a short clip (after a tap, at most 8 s) and sends it to our own `/api/transcribe`, which forwards it to Groq (boundary 4). So on those devices our server now passes the person's voice to a third party; before, only text reached our servers.
2. **Transcript → parser.** Transcripts are untrusted text.
3. **Client → `/api/parse`.** Anyone can call a public URL.
4. **`/api/parse` and `/api/coach` → Gemini, and Groq for the coach and for `/api/transcribe`.** External models; their output is untrusted. Calls have timeouts (parse 8 s; coach 12 s for Gemini then 8 s for Groq when both are set up, 20 s for Gemini alone; transcribe 10 s), and the three endpoints share a project-wide daily ceiling.
5. **Client → Supabase.** Public anon key; RLS is the only gate.

## Residual risks

Audio goes to the browser vendor's recogniser, and on phones where that cannot hear, to Groq through `/api/transcribe` (T20; checked on an iPhone 15 in Safari and Chrome); Supabase free projects may pause; Gemini and Groq free-tier terms may change; email confirmation is on, but many accounts can still be opened (the per-user limits and the daily ceiling bound the damage); confirmation emails can land in spam (Brevo free plan, Gmail sender). All are listed in the README limitations.

## Validation

Mitigations T1 to T5 each map to an automated test (validator unit tests, the RLS isolation suite, and the fake-provider tests of `/api/parse` and `/api/coach`: `api/_lib/parse.test.ts`, `coach.test.ts`, `coachScope.test.ts`, `fallbackProvider.test.ts`) and, for T20, `api/_lib/transcribe.test.ts` and `transcribeProvider.test.ts`. Results are recorded in the [QA report](../qa/ai-qa-report.md).
